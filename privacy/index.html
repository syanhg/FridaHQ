Privacy Policy

Last updated October 15, 2026

MonyCompany Inc. (“we,” “us,” or “our”) operates Frida, a personal agent for iPhone. Frida keeps your conversation, and what it knows about you, on your phone. This policy explains what reaches us, what leaves your phone, who receives it and why, and what you can ask us to do about any of it.

Frida sends what you share with it to the AI services and the advertising partner named in section 3. By signing in to Frida, you agree to this policy and to that.

1. Information we collect

  • Sign in with Apple. Frida offers no other way in. We request your name only, not your email address. Apple also returns a stable identifier for your account. Both are held in the iOS Keychain on your device. When you sign in, Frida shows our server the sign-in token Apple issued, so it can check which Apple account is using Frida. Our server keeps only a one-way digest of that identifier, never the identifier itself, and the date you started using Frida, so your weekly usage limit follows your account rather than your phone. Your weeks are counted from that date.
  • Your phone’s registration. A random identifier Frida creates for your phone, a secret that proves requests come from it, when it last reached our server, and its time zone, which we use to count your week and to time work you schedule.
  • Usage records. For each request Frida makes to an AI model: when it was made, which model answered, how long it took, its size and cost, and the names of the tools it used, such as “weather” or “search”. Never the words of your messages or of Frida’s answers. We keep these for 14 days, to run the weekly usage limit, keep costs in check, and find faults. We also keep monthly and daily totals of how much each phone used.
  • Purchases. If you buy something through Frida, our server keeps what is needed to place and track the order: what you bought, its price, and its status. Your name, email, phone number, and delivery address are kept until the order has been placed or has failed, and never longer than 7 days. If you connect a payment wallet, our server holds the connection so it can request payment when you approve a purchase. If you save a card for shopping, it is kept in our payment partner’s vault, and our server keeps only its brand, last four digits, and expiry date. Card numbers are never stored by us.
  • Your computer. If you use Frida’s computer, our server keeps a record of each job: its title, whether it finished, the summary Frida wrote of what it found, and the names of the files it used and made, for 14 days. The files themselves are described in section 3.
  • Notifications. The token Apple gives Frida to send your phone notifications, so we can tell you when a job on your computer finishes or a reply you are waiting for arrives. If you ask Frida to watch for a reply in Gmail, our server also keeps a one-way digest of your Gmail address, never the address itself, for as long as it watches.
  • Calls to businesses. If Frida calls a business for you, our server keeps the business’s phone number and the call’s reference for 30 days.
  • Templates you share. If you publish a template for other people to use, we keep it, with the name you publish it under, until you withdraw it.
  • Server logs. Our server writes short-lived logs to find faults. They record errors and timings, and the words of shopping searches, but not who made them.
  • Support and access requests. What you write to us, and the address you write from.
  • Diagnostics. Crash and performance reports, if you have allowed your device to share them. These reach us through Apple, aggregated, and do not contain your conversation.

Frida has no cookies and no third-party analytics. It never reads your advertising identifier, and nothing in it follows you across other companies’ apps and websites.

2. What stays on your device

Your conversation, the memories Frida forms about you, the habits it observes, the cards it draws, the work it has scheduled, your reservations, the route of a walk or run you track, and its cache of photographs are written to the app’s private storage on your phone. This includes anything Frida reads for you from an account you connect, which becomes part of the conversation. Your Apple account identifier, your name, the contact and delivery details you save, the cards and travellers you save for flights, and the tokens for the accounts you connect are held in the iOS Keychain on your phone. A saved card’s number and security code open only to your Face ID or passcode, when you pay. We do not keep any of it, apart from what section 1 describes, and we cannot recover any of it for you.

3. What leaves your device, who receives it, and why

Frida needs outside services to answer you. Each receives only what its part of the job requires.

AI services

These are the AI services Frida uses, by name.

  • Fireworks AI runs the AI models that write every answer. It receives your message, the conversation so far, photos and files you add and the words read from them, what Frida remembers about you, the name of your neighbourhood, and the results of anything Frida looked up or read for you, including from accounts you connect. It also runs the agent that works on your computer, and receives what that agent reads there.
  • OpenAI runs voice calls with Frida. During a call it receives your voice, what Frida remembers about you, the recent conversation, and the text of Frida’s answers so they can be spoken.
  • Google (Gemini) makes and edits pictures. It receives the words describing the picture and any photos you give it, including a photo you take of yourself or your room to see a product on it.
  • VOYGR makes phone calls to businesses when you ask Frida to call one. It receives the business’s phone number, your name, what you want from the call, and anything you answer during it. VOYGR records and transcribes the call, and keeps the recording for up to 90 days.
  • Reducto reads documents. When you attach a Word, PowerPoint, or Excel file, or a scanned PDF, the whole file is sent to it so Frida can read its words.
  • Orbit looks people up when you ask Frida to find someone. It receives what you said about the person and any link you gave.

With requests to Fireworks AI and OpenAI, Frida includes an identifier derived one way from your phone or your Apple account, so they can detect abuse. It is never your name or your Apple account identifier itself.

We use these services under terms that do not allow them to train their models on what we send, and we switch off storage of requests wherever a service lets us. Your precise location is never sent to an AI service. It is told the name of your neighbourhood, and nothing finer.

Ads

  • Koah, our advertising partner, shows the ads in Frida. It receives every second question you ask, with Frida’s answer to it, so the ad it may show under them fits what you were talking about. Once Frida has read your email, your calendar, or another account you connect in a conversation, only your questions from that conversation go, never its answers. Koah also receives what an ad needs to be shown and counted: details of your phone such as its model, screen size, iOS version, language and region, connection type, and whether location services are on; an approximate location worked out from your IP address; a random identifier created on your phone for this app; and which ads you saw and tapped. It is never sent your advertising identifier, your precise location, or the memories Frida holds about you, though an answer can mention something Frida remembers. It does not link what it receives to your identity, or use it to follow you across other companies’ apps and websites. Tapping an ad opens the advertiser’s page, which sees your IP address as any website does.

Other services

  • Search, map, and weather services receive a search query, the address of a page, or the name of a place. If you allow location, Apple’s map and weather services receive it, so “nearby” means nearby and a forecast is for where you are. No other service receives your precise location.
  • Your computer. If you use Frida’s computer, the company that hosts it receives each job’s task and the files you give it: files from the conversation, and things you choose from your library. Your email and calendar are never sent to it. Its machines are in the European Union. The files stay on your computer, so later jobs can use them, until it has gone unused for 30 days or you delete your account; they also pass through our server, which deletes them within a day of the job ending. A website you ask it to publish goes to a website host and is public at its address until you take it down.
  • Accounts you connect receive the requests Frida makes on your behalf, with the permission you granted.
  • Shopping and payment partners receive what you are buying, your name, email, phone number, and delivery address, and the payment you approved, so the store can fulfil the order. When a partner completes a checkout on a store’s own website for you, it also receives the store’s page, your instructions, and your answers to any questions the store asks.
  • Restaurant booking partners receive what you are looking for and, when you book, your name, phone number, email, and a card if the restaurant requires one. These go to the restaurant’s booking system.
  • Travel booking partners receive the trip you are searching for and, when you book a flight, each traveller’s name, gender, and date of birth, your email and phone number, your billing address, and the card you pay with, so the airline can issue the ticket.

How these services must protect it

We share your information only with services bound, by their agreements with us or by the terms they offer developers, to protect it at least as well as this policy does: to use it only to provide their service to us, never to sell it, and to keep it secure. What each one keeps, and for how long, is also set out in its own privacy policy.

4. Gmail and Google Calendar

If you connect Gmail, Frida asks Google for permission to read your email and to send email from your account. If you connect Google Calendar, it asks to read your calendars and to change the events on them. It asks for nothing wider: it cannot delete, archive, or label email, or change email already in your mailbox. Google lists each permission on its sign-in screen before you agree.

  • What Frida accesses. Gmail: the messages, conversations, and attachments it searches or opens to answer you, and your mailbox’s address. Google Calendar: your list of calendars and the events on them.
  • How it is used. Only to provide the features you ask for: answering questions about your email and your schedule, showing messages and events in the conversation, sending the replies, forwards, and emails you write in Frida, moving, editing, and deleting your events, answering invitations, creating events that invite the people you name, and showing your Google events alongside the calendar on your phone.
  • Nothing goes out without you. Frida sends an email only when you press Send on it, and waits a few seconds first so you can undo it. It sends a calendar invitation only after you confirm it. It changes an event only when you move, edit, or delete it yourself on a calendar card, or answer an invitation there. When an event has guests, Google tells them about the change or your answer, as Google Calendar does.
  • Where it goes. Frida reads your Google data from your phone, directly from Google, and sends your email and calendar changes from your phone straight to Google. What it reads is sent to Fireworks AI to produce your answer, and during a voice call to OpenAI. It is never sent to Koah or any advertiser, never sent to your computer, and never stored on our servers. An email you send is kept on your phone until Google confirms it has gone, so it still goes if Frida is closed, and a record that it went is kept on your phone for 90 days so it is never sent twice.
  • Pictures in email. When you open an email laid out with pictures, such as a newsletter or a receipt, the pictures load from the sender’s servers, as they do in most mail apps. That can tell the sender the email was opened.
  • What we never do. We do not sell Google user data. We do not use it for advertising or to train AI models, ours or anyone else’s. We do not use it to determine creditworthiness or for lending. Nobody at MonyCompany reads it, unless you ask us to for a specific message, it is needed to investigate abuse or keep Frida secure, or the law requires it.
  • How to revoke access. Tap Disconnect beside Gmail or Calendar in the app, or remove Frida at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Delete Account in the app revokes both.

Frida’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

5. Permissions the app asks for

Each is asked for when it is first needed, never at launch, and Frida works without any of them. Once iOS has an answer on file, only Settings can change it.

  • Calendar and Reminders, to answer questions about your week, put holds on your calendar, move, edit, and delete the events you change on a calendar card, and keep your lists in sync.
  • Location, as described in section 3. Optionally always, so an errand tied to arriving somewhere works with the app closed. While you track a walk or a run, Frida keeps measuring with the phone locked, and iOS shows that it is.
  • Microphone, only while you are on a voice call with Frida.
  • Camera, to read a card you hold up, which is read on your phone and never photographed or sent, and to take a photo of you or your room to see a product on it, which is sent to Google as section 3 describes.
  • Photos, to show your latest photos above the keyboard, and to save a picture Frida made when you press Save. A photo leaves your phone only if you attach it.
  • Motion, to count steps while you track a walk or a run. It stays on your phone.
  • Face ID, to confirm it is you before a payment or a booking. iOS handles this; Frida never sees your face data.
  • Apple Music, to play what you ask for.
  • Notifications, for timers, moments you asked about, replies you are waiting for, and work you scheduled.

6. How we use it

To let you sign in and keep you signed in; to run what you ask Frida to run, including placing orders and bookings you approve; to apply the weekly usage limit; to answer you when you write to us; to fix faults and improve the app; to show ads that fit the conversation they appear in, as section 3 describes; to investigate reports of abuse; and to meet legal obligations.

7. How we share it

  • The services described in section 3, and the company that hosts our server, only for the purposes described there.
  • Other Frida users, who see a template you publish and the name you publish it under.
  • Apple, which handles sign-in, notifications, maps, weather, app distribution, and any diagnostics you have chosen to share.
  • Legal authorities, where the law requires it, and an acquiring party in a merger, acquisition, or sale of assets.

We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. The ads in Frida are chosen from the conversation they appear in and your approximate location, not from what you do in other apps and websites.

8. Connected accounts

You choose which accounts to connect. Each is connected through the provider’s own sign-in, so you authorize with that provider and grant only the permissions you are shown. The tokens are stored in your device’s Keychain and used from your device. A payment wallet is the exception: our server holds its connection, as section 1 describes. You can disconnect an account at any time in the app, or revoke it in that provider’s own settings.

9. Your choices, retention, and deletion

The AI services and ads are part of how Frida works, so the way to stop sending to them is to stop using Frida: sign out, or delete your account as described below.

Delete Account in the app (open your library, tap the person button, then Delete Account) erases your conversation and every thread, the memories and habits Frida formed, work it had scheduled, your saved details, cards, and travellers, the grants for every connected account, anything it put on your Lock Screen, email still waiting to be sent, its notifications, and its cache of photographs.

It also deletes everything our server holds for your phone: your payment wallet connection, which is revoked with the wallet provider; a card saved for shopping, which is deleted from our payment partner’s vault; every record of your purchases; your computer, with every file on it and every copy of it, and the websites it published; the files of its jobs; your usage records; and your notification token. If your phone is offline at that moment, this happens the next time Frida opens with a connection.

One thing outlives it, briefly: the digest of your Apple account identifier, the date you started using Frida, and how much of the week’s usage limit you had used, so that deleting your account and signing in again does not start a fresh week. It is deleted within ten days. Server logs expire on their own within weeks.

Deleting your account cannot reach what the services in section 3 already received; each keeps it as its own policy describes, such as a call recording kept up to 90 days. Deleting the app removes what is on your phone but cannot reach our server, so use Delete Account first, or write to us and we will delete it. We keep support correspondence until we have finished acting on it.

Calendar events and reminders Frida wrote for you stay in your own apps, and email you sent stays in your mailbox. Deleting an account is not licence to reach into them.

10. Security

We use encryption in transit, keep credentials and tokens in the iOS Keychain, and keep your content on your device rather than on a server of ours. No method of transmission or storage is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security. Because your data lives on your device, the passcode, Face ID, and backup settings on that device are part of protecting it.

11. Your rights

You can delete what is on your phone yourself, as described in section 9. You can also ask us to access, correct, export, or delete personal information we hold. Depending on where you live you may have further rights over how your data is used. Write to alex@mony.company and we will respond within a reasonable period.

12. Children’s privacy

Frida is not directed to children, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 13. If you believe a child has given us information, write to us and we will remove it.

Information that reaches us, and requests your device makes to the services in section 3, may be processed in the United States, in the European Union where your computer runs, or anywhere those services operate. Where the law requires it, we apply appropriate safeguards to those transfers.
